Open AI Dividend

How people could share cash earned by an AI service.

Each eligible person has one nontransferable claim per period, worth the same as every other person’s claim.

A founder, a funder and someone admitted on the cutoff date each receive the same amount. A future claim cannot be sold, pledged or pooled. Once paid, recipients can spend or give away the money as they choose.

Where the money comes from

A pilot would need a signed agreement dedicating a fixed percentage of cash collected by a named service. It would define sales through affiliates, taxes, refunds, fees and reserves before the period begins. An independent reviewer would reconcile the sales journal, contract and bank deposits.

Funding example. At 2% of €100,000 in eligible receipts, €2,000 is due. A deposit of €1,800 leaves a €200 gap. The period stays on hold until the discrepancy is resolved. Verified zero receipts generate a zero contribution for that period.

What each person receives

In a separate example, €100 is available for three eligible people. One person is still confirming a payment route and counts in full.

Once the payment route is confirmed, the reserved amount can be released once. Before retrying an uncertain transfer, reconcile it against the payment journal. A settled transfer must never be repeated.

Who can change the rules?

Participants have equal voting weight on procedural changes. An independent reviewer checks that the equal claim survives each change. New procedures take effect in a future period. An emergency stop can pause transfers but cannot redirect funds or add recipients.

Who can check the result?

Participants need a way to challenge an error. Reviewers need the evidence behind each calculation.

Eligibility and appeal

Publish the pilot’s eligibility criteria, cutoff and appeal process in advance. Offer free assisted access and alternative evidence for people without standard documents. Two people sharing a bank account keep separate claims. A representative acts for the beneficiary. A disputed identity match requires human review.

A credible unresolved eligibility claim holds the period open because it could change everyone’s amount. If an omitted person is recognised after closing, a separate correction reserve should fund their equal claim. Record any unfunded correction as outstanding.

Three review outcomes

  1. PASS. Funds match the agreement, the eligible group is settled, and payable amounts, personal reserves and rounding carry reconcile to the available balance.
  2. HOLD. A missing deposit or unresolved eligibility case pauses the period. An uncertain payment route pauses only that person’s payment once eligibility is settled.
  3. BLOCKER. A second claim for one person, a founder bonus or a retroactive rule change prevents release. Link a corrected assessment to the previous one.

Record the policy version, cutoff, source evidence references, headcount, amount per person, reserves, carry and decision. Let participants inspect their own receipt. Publish aggregates only where individuals cannot be inferred. Auditors need independent access to primary records. A matching digest can detect a changed receipt but cannot establish the truth of its inputs.

What a pilot would still need

The offline model uses synthetic inputs. A cash pilot requires a willing contributor, a responsible operator, verified funds, an independent appeals body and legal review. Owning part of an AI business would also require defined assets and enforceable ownership rights. Under this proposal, equity ownership, compute access and a claim on cash are separate arrangements.
